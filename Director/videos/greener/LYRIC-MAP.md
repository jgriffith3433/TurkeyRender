## Performance direction (J, 2026-11-06)

"The character needs to act like a 2000s singer in a music video that fits the lyrics. Like a dance and song while looking at the camera and singing."

He is the star of a 2000s pop video, with total confidence. This is the main thing in the video, above props. The invisible dress stays as part of how he moves.

- **He looks into the lens** whenever he sings on screen. Sings every line with real mouth shapes. Eyes alive, brows moving, a small smirk, a slow head tilt, a look over the shoulder, a hair flick.
- **Performance shots.** Between story shots, cut to him singing and moving straight to camera: walking into the lens, leaning in on a close-up, hand on hip, hand to chest, pointing at the viewer.
- **A real dance.** Simple 2000s pop moves on the beat: hip sway, step-touch, shoulder shimmy, body roll, turn, arm up. Butlers behind him in formation, doing the same moves in sync.
- **A signature hook move** on every "I'm your gateway drug, baby": the same move each time, easy to remember, finishing with a point at the lens. Both choruses, four times in all.
- **Lyric gestures:** "little secret" finger to lips. "weakness" hand on heart. "call me what you like" shrug and hair flick. "It's not a big deal" a small dismissive wave. "look at you" point at the lens. "something new" taste the blade. "didn't know" eye roll. "mind my manners" prim curtsy. "get some sleep" hands under cheek. "let go" hand opening. "Oh, darling" chin down, eyes up.
- **The dip** stays still and small. It only works because everything else moves.
- **Dance must never look broken:** J called the TK Shock dance "so so bad", because it was built by hand. Whole-body mocap clips only, warped to the beat with `warpTo`, watched at 30 fps. No `reach` or `pose` to build a dance.

## How we shoot it (2026-11-06, replaces shot-by-shot polish)

J: iterations aren't proving good enough. Stop spreading effort over 80 shots. Shoot it like a real music video.

1. **One performance take.** He sings and dances the whole of a section once, in one set, as one continuous take. Animation gets built and judged once. Cards already keep a performance running across cuts when it has the same performance on the same mark, so every camera sees the same take.
2. **Many cameras on that take.** Wide symmetrical, medium, close-up to lens, low hero, a smooth track. The cut is then just choosing cameras on the beat. New shots cost nothing and can't break the animation.
3. **Slice first.** Chorus 1, bars 29-40 (48-69 s), in the hall, at full quality, with butlers dancing in sync. Nothing else gets polish until this is great. Output `videos/greener/slice.mp4`, 1280x720.
4. **Then repeat it.** Chorus 2 reuses the chorus take. Verses get their own take in their own set, and story gags cut in on top, about one in four shots.
