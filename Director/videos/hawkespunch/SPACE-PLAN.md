## Why p5 failed
- Ground clips were floated, then patched: a reclined "hold the ball" pose for the legs put everyone in an invisible dentist chair, torsos tipped 45° away from each other.
- Shots were placed one at a time with no blocking plan, so the bodies faced the lens or away from their partner.
- The gags were ground gags (a boop, a kick) with no zero-g physics. In space the physics is the joke.

## Body rules (every space body, every frame)
1. **Base = the neutral float (NBP).** Arms floating up and out from the shoulders, elbows soft, knees and hips bent, feet pointing down, spine slightly rounded, head level. This is a keyed base clip (`zg_neutral`) with slow breathing, not a generated or ground clip. Never seated, never reclined, never straight-legged.
2. **Acting is upper body on top of the float** (AvatarMask upper or arms). The legs stay in the float and react only through physics: they trail, swing and overshoot.
3. **The head is level and acting**: a gaze to the partner or the lens, limited to 40°. No clip may tip the face to the sky or the floor.
4. **Always drifting.** 2-5 cm/s and 1-3°/s of rotation, never perfectly still.
5. **Nothing changes speed without a push.** Every move starts from a push (off a planet, off the other man, or a throw) and the body keeps going. Every push moves both sides, the lighter one more.
6. **Timing is about 1.5x slower and smoother than ground acting**, except the instant of a push or impact.
7. **Hands relaxed** (curl 0.4) unless gripping.

## Blocking (the overhead plan)
- World: the camera side is -z, and Jupiter sits huge behind them at +z, screen right. The playground line runs along x at z 1700.
- **The line:** HIM is always screen left (-x), the DOUBLE always screen right (+x). **Every camera stays on the -z side of the line.** The one swap is the push-apart in gag 3, shown on screen.
- **Facing:** in two-shots they face each other turned about 30° toward the lens (him yaw about 120°, the double about 240°, where 180 = lens). In singles the speaker faces his partner off-frame, with the eyeline 15-25° off the lens. Only the ad turn (8.6) faces the lens.
- **Screen up = their up.** Heads at the top of frame, except during a spin gag.
- **Shot sizes:**
  - dialogue and reactions: MCU or medium, chest up;
  - physics gags: full shot, side-on, the whole body;
  - wides: only 5.1 (the establishing shot) and the chase;
  - every gag shot holds long enough to see the push, the travel and the result.

## The scenes, as zero-g physics comedy

### 5. Arrival (keep the timing)
5.1 **WIDE, establishing.** He sails in from screen left, still going, rotating slowly. He can't stop. He flails to stop, which only adds a slow spin (limb swings rotate you). The spin settles with him upright and facing us, just past centre. The scream cuts dead.
5.2 **MCU, lit.** Silence: "Silence." ... "Nope. More music."
5.3 **MCU, push in.** "Wait. Space... She's up here." He pulls out the drawing.

### 6. The search: the turn gag (suspense, rule of three)
6.1 **MEDIUM, him, the drawing in hand.** He wants to look behind him, but there's nothing to push on. He tries to turn and windmills his arms, rotating slowly round. **As he turns, the ghost drifts across behind him the other way, always just out of his view.** We see it; he doesn't.
6.2 He finishes the turn and faces away from us. Nothing there. He windmills back. The ghost peeks over his shoulder, then ducks behind the ringed ball. (Second time: the pattern.)
6.3 **MCU.** A tap on his shoulder. He freezes. "...Space ghost?"
6.4 **TWO-SHOT, side.** He turns (a small push off the drawing hand's swing) and it's HIM in a red tee, grinning. He looks at the drawing, then the double, then the drawing. "...no."
6.5 **CLOSE.** The double boops his nose. "Boop!" **Physics:** the boop pushes him gently backwards, and the double drifts back too. He drifts away from the double with an offended face, can't stop, and slowly leaves frame left. Hold on the empty space. He drifts back in? No: the double laughs.

### 7. The chase: Newton is the joke
7.1 **FULL SHOT, side.** The double plants both feet on the ringed ball and **kicks off**. He shoots away right; the ball shoots away left, past HIM. "Ha!"
7.2 **MEDIUM, him.** "Hey! Are you her?!" He looks for something to push off: grabs the passing ball and pushes it. He goes right, slowly. (He did it wrong: too slow.)
7.3 **WIDE CHASE, side, left to right, Jupiter behind.** Both glide in straight lines. The double drifts past a little planet. **Gag 1, the scare:** the double ducks behind the little planet.
7.4 **MEDIUM.** He glides past it. **CLOSE TWO-SHOT:** "BOO!" "Aah!" His startle flails his arms and legs, **which sets him spinning slowly**. He keeps rotating through the next line.
7.5 **MCU, him rotating** (upside-down by the end), embarrassed. The double laughs, upright.
7.6 **FULL SHOT, side. Gag 2, the push:** the double stops his spin, plants a foot on his backside and **shoves**. HIM shoots off right and the double shoots off left. Both leave frame. "You're it!" comes from off-screen left.
7.7 **MEDIUM. Gag 3, the throw.** He glides, grabs a little red planet (Mars) on the way, looks at it, then back. "Oh, it's on."
7.8 **FULL SHOT, side, built for it.** He throws Mars. **The recoil sends him backwards** out of frame left at the same time (Newton). Mars sails right and hits the double in the gut. The double folds around it and drifts away right with it.
7.9 **MEDIUM, the double, drifting, hugging Mars.** Winded: "Right in the... Mars."
7.10 **CLOSE, the double.** A slow evil grin. He lets Mars go, reaches out of frame and comes back with the little blue-and-green planet, winding up. (Only an arm move: no clip may lift his whole body.)
7.11 **INSERT.** It's Earth.
7.12 **MEDIUM TWO-SHOT.** HIM drifts back into frame (still from his recoil) and sees it. "Dude. Dude. That's the Earth." "I know. I know."
7.13 **MEDIUM TWO-SHOT, one shot.** They both hold the Earth and gently let go. **It keeps drifting slowly away** (nothing stops in space). Their hands hover, then drop. "Sorry, everybody." "Nobody saw that."

### 8. The stop: momentum makes it sweet
8.1 **TWO-SHOT, side.** Out of breath (breathing on the chest and shoulders only), they drift slowly toward each other. The forehead bump is the cold viewer's missing "forehead contact": **they bump gently, foreheads**, and start drifting apart.
8.2 **MCU, him.** As they drift apart he grabs the double's sleeve to stay close. "Are you... the space ghost?"
8.3 **MCU, the double.** Gentle. "No, man. I'm you."
8.4 **MCU, him, slow push in.** He looks at his face. "You do have a face." He looks at the drawing, shrugs, crumples it, and lets go of it. The ball of paper drifts away slowly.
8.5 **WIDE TWO-SHOT.** Side by side. The crumpled drawing drifts behind them, and **the ghost drifts in, picks it up, smooths it out, and looks at it**. Neither notices. He starts to turn; the double turns his chin back: "Hey. Focus."
8.6 **TWO-SHOT to lens.** Both turn to camera (a small mutual push, so they rotate together): "Are you feeling like there's two of you?" Into the ad.

### Button (space part)
B.1 **WIDE.** He sails in from left, can't stop, and the double stops him by grabbing his collar (a hard cut on the grab). The double holds out two cans: "Same time tomorrow?"
B.2 **TWO-SHOT.** He takes one. They clink and drink. **Physics:** the clink sets them drifting slowly apart, both still drinking. The ghost waves behind them with the smoothed-out drawing.

## What's needed to build it
- **`zg_neutral`:** the NBP base clip, keyed by hand from the research numbers (clipderive or a keyed humanoid clip): breathing, a slow limb float, and a loop. This is the most important asset.
- **Physics helpers**, as cues or a small runtime helper:
  - a push gives two bodies opposite velocities by mass;
  - a spin is an angular velocity on the root with damping 0;
  - a drift is a constant velocity.
  - Root motion only, deterministic.
- **Upper-body acting clips** from the catalog: talk, laugh, startle arms, throw arms (gm_zg_throw, arms), search (gm_zg_search, arms).
- **Contact moments** (the forehead bump, the sleeve grab, the collar grab, the foot on the backside, the Earth in both hands) are sold by the cut and a post-Animator reach. The motion check's contact gate still applies.
