Sound: there is no Whisper on this machine, and I didn't use Gen's `audio.transcribe` because that's a paid GPU job. Treat every "the line says" here as a guess. A real audience would hear the lines; I mostly couldn't.

## What happened

A clinic waiting room with a reception desk, and the receptionist is a live turkey. A young man in a purple T-shirt waits on the chairs, then stares up at the ceiling with his mouth open. With no transition he is sitting on an exam table, and a long-haired woman in a white/grey shirt is the doctor. He talks for a long time with lots of hand-waving: I gather he can't stop hearing music, can't sleep or eat, and is in love with a faceless ghost in space. The doctor mostly looks down at her desk. Suddenly she is standing in front of him and tosses a small red thing (I think a can) that I couldn't really read. More talking in close-ups. A "HAWKES PUNCH" poster appears on the wall behind her. She puts a fist to her mouth, possibly drinking, then winds up and punches him. He blows up through the ceiling, flies into the sky and out into space.

In space he is suddenly bigger than the Earth. Then a man who looks exactly like him, in a red shirt, appears behind him. They square off and fight: grappling, a kick, the red one knocked into an asteroid field, onto a moon, past Saturn. Then they throw planets at each other (Mars, then Earth). They end up holding the Earth between them, face to face, talking for a long time. Hard cut to a bright TV-commercial set: both men on stools beside a giant Hawkes Punch can, pitching it. A crawl lists the side effects, which are what we just watched. "Ask your doctor" flashes for half a second. We cut back to the man on the exam table looking up, then black, then a single can on the clinic floor, and it ends.

The loudest moments of the film (-11.5 at 1:32.5, -12 at 1:13) are no louder than ordinary dialogue peaks (-13 to -15). **No sound effect clearly towers over the voices.** The punch, the launch, the planet hits and the kick feel under-sold.
- The space fight (1:30-2:20) is under continuous talk. I couldn't tell whether that talk was the two men, a narrator or the commercial voice-over bleeding in. Long talk under small, far-off figures means you can't tell who is speaking.
- The side-effects voice-over (about 3:00-3:15) carries most of the explaining. If you miss it (fast, under the picture), the space section stays unexplained.
- Lines vs. picture: the man's "can't stop listening to music" has no music anywhere in the film. If the joke is that we never hear it, it doesn't land; it just sounds like a claim.

## Jokes

**Landed:**
- **The turkey receptionist** (0:00-0:07). Instant and absurd. It's then dropped, which wastes it.
- **"Hawkes Punch" is a literal punch** that launches him into orbit (1:12-1:22). The single best idea. The poster sets it up just in time, but the punch itself is too fast and too smeared to get the laugh it deserves.
- **The commercial reveal** (2:40), with a side-effects crawl that recaps everything we just saw ("sudden flight, meeting yourself, throwing planets"). The funniest structural turn. It retroactively explains the double, if you can read the crawl.
- **"ONE A DAY UNTIL IT STOPS"** on the can, and (from what I could hear) "if you're allergic to punches, your doctor will punch you". Good copy.

**Didn't land:**
- **The long opening monologue** about music and a faceless space ghost: too much talk, too little picture, and nothing to look at.
- **The space fight:** too far away, too many tiny figures, no clear who-hit-whom. Planets as weapons is a good idea, shot as specks.
- **The double:** unexplained on arrival, identical face, no reaction. It doesn't read as "meeting yourself" until the ad tells you.
- **The Earth-holding conversation:** static and long. The payoff (he's the space ghost? the ghost has a face?) didn't come through.
- **The ending** (back to the exam room, black, a can on the floor): no button. The film ends on a shrug.

## Top 10 things an audience would not like (worst first)

1. **The space section is 80 seconds of specks and confusion** (1:23-2:40). Scale changes every shot, figures are tiny, the background colour flips, and who-hits-whom is unreadable. People will check out.
2. **The double appears with no setup and the same face** (1:30). The audience can't tell whether he's a new character, a clone or a model reuse until a crawl explains it two minutes later.
3. **Too much talk over too little picture.** About 3 minutes of nearly continuous dialogue with near-static close-ups (0:23-0:55, 0:58-1:12, 2:23-2:40, 2:52-3:02).
4. **The punch, the film's title gag, goes by in half a second** with no clear "she drinks it" and no big sound, so the best joke is under-sold.
5. **The ending has no payoff.** We go back to the exam room, then black, then a can rolls. Viewers will ask "that's it?"
6. **Empty lower-third bars and a blank pink box** in the commercial look broken, like a render that lost its text.
7. **Dead-silent audio dropouts** at 1:23-1:26.5 and 3:17.5-3:19 sound like a mistake.
8. **The two people in the clinic never look at each other.** The doctor stares at her desk, the patient stares at the ceiling, and she teleports between sitting and standing.
9. **The look of the characters keeps changing:** different hair and lighting on the man from 0:58, yellow arms, mangled hands on the globe, different faces in the commercial. It reads as cheap and confusing.
10. **The turkey receptionist, the best opening image, is abandoned**, and nothing in the film connects to it.

## What the story seems to be about

A lovesick patient goes to the doctor, gets a dose of "Hawkes Punch" (literally a punch), and is launched into space, where he fights himself with planets. It turns out to be an ad listing those as side effects. That's my best guess from picture and fragments; the "music" and "faceless space ghost" thread never resolved for me.
