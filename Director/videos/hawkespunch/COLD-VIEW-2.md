## What happened

1. An empty clinic lobby, with a reception desk and a white turkey behind it.
2. A man in a purple T-shirt sits in the waiting area. We get a quick poster: "HAWKES PUNCH. Ask your doctor."
3. The turkey turns its head. The man stands up, stretches his arm, and we cut to an exam room.
4. In the exam room he talks a lot while a long-haired doctor in a white T-shirt sits at a desk writing and never looks up. As far as I could make out, music is stuck in his head even in silence, and he's in love with a ghost in space who has no face.
5. He gazes up at the ceiling dreamily.
6. We see a framed "Golden Gloves Champion, Heavyweight" certificate on the wall, with a "DR. HALLORAN" nameplate stuck over it.
7. The doctor gets up and hands him a can of Hawkes Punch. There's a close-up of him holding it.
8. They talk. She looks at him calmly while he looks off and talks.
9. Suddenly she winds up and punches him. He flies backwards out through the window and up out of the building, trailing white streaks.
10. The turkey at reception looks up.
11. He flies up through the clouds, the sky goes dark, and he's floating in space in front of a huge Jupiter-type planet, with a small Saturn, Mars and Earth all painted on or next to it.
12. He floats and looks around. The sound cuts out completely for three seconds.
13. A second man appears: the same face and hair in a red T-shirt. They circle each other.
14. They throw Mars back and forth like a ball. The red one catches the Earth, it bounces off his head or arms, and he tosses it around.
15. They grab each other's forearms and hold, face to face, for about 15 seconds.
16. At 2:44 the camera pulls back and it's a TV studio set: lights, a boom mic, a pastel backdrop, a giant Hawkes Punch can. Both men now sit on stools and pitch the drink to camera. Lower thirds read "A REAL DOCTOR / Not looking up" (over a shot of the doctor) and "IS IT RIGHT FOR YOU / Ask your doctor".
17. An "Important Safety Information" card lists the side effects, which are exactly the things we just saw, ending with "If you are allergic to punches, your doctor will punch you."
18. End card: "HAWKES PUNCH, Ask your doctor". Then "THE NEXT DAY".
19. He's back on the exam table with the same doctor. He looks up dreamily and says something like "Well, I met someone." She says "Same time tomorrow."
20. We cut back to space: he arrives through a ring, the red double is holding Mars, and both are holding cans.
21. The film ends on a can rolling across the waiting-room floor under the words "HAWKES PUNCH".

## Who and where

The clinic geography is the best part: lobby, then exam room, with the window behind the exam table that he's later punched through. Moments I didn't know who or where:

- **0:00-0:08:** the turkey is too small in the wide to register. I didn't know there was a turkey until its close-up, and I never learned whether it's the receptionist.
- **0:10-0:12:** the man stands and flexes. I didn't know if he was being called in, if his arm was hurt, or what.
- **0:14-1:10:** often unclear who was speaking. The doctor's lips barely move, and some of her lines may play over his face.
- **1:10:** at the wind-up, the low angle puts the doctor alone in frame with a sliver of his head. Their spatial relation (is he in reach?) isn't clear.
- **1:11:** at the impact frame I couldn't tell who was who; it was a white smear.
- **1:12-1:13:** the exterior building shot: I didn't know if the flying dot was him.
- **1:21 onward, scale:** him, the planets, Mars and Earth have no consistent scale. He's sometimes standing *on* Jupiter's face, sometimes floating in front of it.
- **1:34-1:44 and 2:38-2:40:** the white capsule. Is it the ghost? I couldn't tell. It never interacts with anyone.
- **1:44, the red double:** no entrance. I didn't know who he was. Same face as the patient, so either the patient himself, a twin, or the ghost in human form. The card later suggests "meeting yourself", but then whom did he fall in love with: the ghost, or himself?
- **1:50-2:44, screen direction:** in the space section, purple and red swap sides between the wide and close shots (purple frame-left in some wides, frame-right in the two-shot close-ups at 2:27-2:44). It's hard to track who's who without the shirt colours.
- **2:45:** the studio: is this real? Did the space section happen, or was it always a commercial being filmed? Then the doctor in the ad's lower third is the same real doctor.
- **3:19:** whether he's back in space for real or imagining it.
- **3:26:** the waiting-room floor: whose can, and why there.

## Setups and payoffs

Paid off:
- The poster ("Ask your doctor") becomes the ad tagline and end card. Paid.
- The Golden Gloves certificate becomes the punch. Paid; you anticipate it.
- The doctor not looking up becomes the "A REAL DOCTOR / Not looking up" lower third. Paid; it's a good callback.
- "Music even in silence" becomes the 3 s silent dropout and the "music in total silence" side effect. Sort of paid, but the dropout sounds like a bug.
- "In love with a ghost in space" becomes the space section and the "falling in love with a space ghost" side effect. Paid in the text only; on screen the ghost is invisible or ambiguous.
- The window behind the exam table is where he exits. Paid.
- Every side effect on the card points back to something shown (flight, meeting yourself, throwing planets, holding Earth). Effective as a list joke.

Shown and never paid:
- The turkey receptionist: two shots, then nothing. Not in the ending (we end in the lobby without it).
- The man's arm flex at 0:10.
- The can handed over at 0:45: he never drinks it on screen. So is Hawkes Punch the drink or the punch? The film implies the punch *is* the treatment, but the can then shows up in space and in the ad, and the drink's role is never clear.
- The white capsule in space.
- "Forehead contact" (side effect) never clearly happens.
- "She doesn't have a face": it never pays visually (the red double has a face; the capsule has none but nobody reacts to it).
- The window glass shatter: never seen again (no broken window the next day).

## Suspense

Yes, once. The Golden Gloves insert at 0:42 tells me the doctor is a boxer, so through the 25 s of talking before the punch I expected her to hit him. That worked as anticipation, but the talking scene is so long and flat that the anticipation ran out before the payoff. The poster ("Ask your doctor"), plus the title "Punch", primes the same joke. After the punch there's no suspense at all: the space section has no goal (find the ghost? get home?), so nothing to anticipate. The "next day" coda sets up "same time tomorrow" (I expected a second punch), but it never shows it.

## Film grammar faults

- **No clear inciting action at the punch:** the wind-up (0.8 s) and impact (1 s, a washed-out white smear) are both too short and unreadable. The biggest beat in the film is the least legible.
- **No reaction from the doctor after the punch:** we cut to the turkey, which is good, but we never see the doctor's satisfaction or deadpan. Action without its owner's reaction.
- **Exterior launch shot (1:12):** scale jump. The building is tiny and the "man" is a dark square. Nothing reads.
- **Red double appears without an entrance:** an effect without a cause.
- **Screen direction flips** between wide and close shots in space.
- **Scale chaos in space:** Jupiter is a backdrop, yet characters stand on it; Mars and Earth are beach balls. There's no rule, so nothing reads as impressive.
- **Shots too long:** patient monologue (0:14-0:44); doctor's static close-up 1:00-1:06; floating close-up 1:24-1:34; forearm hold 2:22-2:44 (20 s, the worst); upward gaze 0:39-0:42.
- **Shots too short:** poster 0:07 (1.5 s for a lot of small text); doctor wind-up 1:10; the "A REAL DOCTOR" lower-third subline is unreadable at this size.
- **Lip sync / speaker ambiguity:** the doctor's face barely animates, so who's talking is unclear in the exam room.
- **The 3 s digital silence (1:21.5-1:24.5)** sounds like broken audio, not a choice. With no music elsewhere, there's no contrast that would make "silence" a joke.
- **Studio reveal (2:44):** good, but the camera move is so fast that the first frame (black void, flats at odd angles) reads as a glitch before it resolves.
- **Ending shot:** an empty floor with a can, no character, no reaction. A shot where nothing reads.

## Jokes

Landed:
- **The side-effects card** (3:01-3:11): "throwing planets, briefly holding the Earth ... falling in love with a space ghost ... If you are allergic to punches, your doctor will punch you." The funniest thing in the film, and it works because of the deadpan list.
- **The studio reveal** at 2:44: it was a commercial all along. A real laugh of recognition.
- **"A REAL DOCTOR / Not looking up"**: a good callback, though tiny on screen.
- **The turkey's reaction to the punch** (1:13): good deadpan cutaway.
- **The punch itself as a concept** (Golden Gloves doctor punches a patient into space): funny idea, but the execution is a smear.

Didn't land:
- **The patient's monologue:** too long, hard to hear, no visual gag to support it. The music and space-ghost problems might be funny lines, but they're buried.
- **The space section** (1:21-2:44, almost 1.5 minutes): no jokes I could see as jokes. Planet catch is mildly amusing but too far away; the long grapple is just confusing.
- **The turkey:** an absurd image with no joke built on it.
- **"Same time tomorrow"**: a good button if heard clearly, but mushy audio and the film then cuts away to something else, so it doesn't land as the final line.
- **The final can roll:** no joke.

## Top 10 things an audience would not like (worst first)

1. **The space section is long and aimless** (1:21-2:44, about 85 s): no goal, no jokes on screen, a 20 s forearm hold, and it only makes sense after the side-effects card explains it.
2. **The punch is unreadable:** a sub-second wind-up and a washed-out white-flash impact. The title beat is the blurriest moment.
3. **The red double appears from nowhere, unexplained:** same face as the hero, no entrance, no reaction. Is he me? Is he the ghost? Who is he in love with?
4. **The opening exam-room scene drags:** 30 s of one guy talking in near-identical framing, then 20 s more of static talking heads before the punch.
5. **The "space ghost" never appears in a way anyone can see.** The love story the dialogue promises has no visible love interest.
6. **The dialogue is hard to follow** (mushy voices and no visual support), so the setup jokes (music in silence, faceless ghost) don't reach the audience before they're paid off.
7. **The ending is muddled:** after a proper ad ending, "The Next Day" brings a coda that goes back to space without showing how, then ends on an empty floor with a can.
8. **The 3 s hard digital silence at 1:21** sounds like a broken file.
9. **The turkey and arm flex in the opening:** set up and abandoned, so the opening feels random rather than absurd-on-purpose.
10. **Scale and screen direction in space are inconsistent:** planets as balls, men standing on Jupiter, characters swapping sides. It's hard to follow who's where.

## What the story is about

A lovesick patient asks his doctor for help, gets punched into space by a boxer doctor as "treatment", meets and falls for (apparently) himself, and the whole thing turns out to be a commercial for a drug whose side effects are exactly what we saw. I can only tell that last part because the safety card spells it out; the pictures alone don't say what he wanted or what changed for him.
