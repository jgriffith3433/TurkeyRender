# The polish bar

J: "Instead of looking at it and thinking it's close enough, look at it and think how does this compare to other music videos. Pace, timing, etc. We're not going for rough, we're going for polished."

The question is never "is this better than last time?" It is: **would this sit beside a high-end animated music video on a major label's channel without looking amateur?** If not, it isn't done. Name the specific gap and fix it.

Every review compares against that bar, in writing, shot by shot. "Fine" and "close enough" are not verdicts.

## Pace
- The energy curve matches the song. Plan cuts per bar for each section and count them in the render.
- Quiet sections earn their length with a developing event. The eye gets something new at least every 2 bars.
- Vary shot length. A run of equal-length shots reads as a slideshow.
- The last drop beats the first in scale, speed and invention.

## Timing
- Big hits land on the exact frame of the transient. Check by stepping frames around it, not by eye at speed.
- Anticipation comes before the hit: a held breath, a lean, a dark frame.
- Follow-through after it: dust, sway, settling, ringing light.
- Actions start early enough to peak on the beat, rather than starting on it.

## Animation
- Every move has weight: ease, overshoot, settle. No linear moves except where it's a style choice.
- Secondary motion: hair, shirt, feathers, things shaken by things.
- Clips blend. Feet plant, hands reach what they touch, eyes lead the head.
- No sliding, popping, floating or intersecting through objects.

## Transitions
- Every cut is motivated: on action, on a match of shape or motion, on the beat, or as a reveal.
- Prefer match cuts, wipes by foreground objects, whip pans and light-outs over plain cuts between big changes.
- Screen direction and eyelines stay consistent, so you always know where he, the TV and the threat are.

## Image
- Composition holds as a still: clear subject, depth, foreground, background.
- A designed colour script per part, not random colour.
- Lighting is motivated and shapes the subject. Nothing flat, blown or crushed unless it's a deliberate hit.
- Effects punctuate; they never hide weak content.

## Finish
- Sound design on impacts, clicks, slams and birds, sitting under the song.
- No visible engine artefacts: shadow acne, aliasing, popping, z-fighting, clipped geometry, default skies.
- Check at full resolution and at speed before calling anything done.
