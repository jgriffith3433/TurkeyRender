# How others do it: research for a director-from-text pipeline

Researched 2026-11-06. Companion to `Director/PIPELINE.md`, which says what went wrong on TK Shock. This file says how other teams solved the same problems and what to adopt. Findings are ranked by likely effect on quality and on fewer iterations. The ranking is my judgement; nobody has measured these on this pipeline.

## How to read the labels

**Evidence**
- **PROVEN:** used on shipped productions or games.
- **RESEARCH:** works in papers or demos, with no production record for this kind of use.
- **SPECULATIVE:** my own extension, or an idea that nobody here has tried. Test it before relying on it.

**How the source was read**
- **[read]:** I fetched the page itself.
- **[excerpt]:** I saw only search-result excerpts. Check these before quoting them as fact.

## The short answer

TK Shock's weakness is the level the score is written at, more than any one tool.

Measured on `Director/acts/` today: 2,927 `do` cues against about 130 `shot` cues, roughly 23 low-level cues per shot. One cue in three is bookkeeping (`hide` 561, `show` 410). The two largest acts (`05-inside.json` at 371 KB and `03-the-pull.json` at 181 KB) are not hand-written. They are generated by `tools/part7.mjs` and `tools/p34_build.mjs`, and the Part 4 and Part 6 work sits in more one-off scripts. So agents already needed a higher-level language, and each built a private one that nobody can reuse.

Every comparable production separates four things that TK Shock mixes in one list of cues:

| Layer | Answers | Who owns it elsewhere |
|---|---|---|
| Cut | Which shots, in what order, how long | Editorial |
| Performance | What the body does, as one whole take | Mocap or animation |
| Shot | Where the camera and actors sit | Layout |
| Look | Light, grade, style | Lighting and comp |

Each layer can change without touching the others, and that is what makes a pivot cheap. The closest published match to this project is Mind-of-Director: Unity, Timeline, a Mixamo library, parameterized camera templates and agents. It reports about 25 minutes per idea for previs. Its stated limit is the one TK Shock hit: expression is capped by the animation library and retargeting. That is why finding 2 ranks so high.

## Corrections to what we assumed

Three things I found change the current plan or current docs.

1. **Hawkes Gen already has most of the AI motion path.** `hawkesgen/docs/3d-characters.md` documents `motion.from-text` (Kimodo-SOMA-RP-v1.2, HY-Motion fallback, retarget through `blender.character`, `takes` 1 to 8, judged with `library.judge`) and a CC0 Quaternius motion library (`motion:<pack>/<clip>`). Its doc lists a clip at about $0.30 to $1. The blocker is narrow: `src/tools/motion.ts` is `server: 'ssh'`, and the SSH servers went with AWS. This is a port to RunPod or Modal, not a new integration. The port has to carry two requirements from Gen's doc: 60 GB of free disk on first install, and a Hugging Face token granted the gated Llama 3 text encoder, without which Kimodo is skipped. `AGENTS.md` is right that it is unavailable today, but it is not a dead end.
2. **Kimodo needs less GPU than the headline number.** The 17 GB figure drops under 3 GB with `TEXT_ENCODER_DEVICE=cpu`, per its README [read]. It is still not an integrated-graphics job, but it fits a cheap rented GPU.
3. **Unreal is not worth moving to.** Motion Matching and Sequencer are ahead of Unity, but moving would throw away the compiler, the checks and the engine, and this PC has no GPU to run it well. The ideas transfer. The engine does not need to.

## Tier 1: highest impact. Do these before the next video.

### 1. Raise the score to intent: shots, blocking and templates that compile down

**Evidence: PROVEN for the pattern (Virtual Cinematographer, shot templates, Mind-of-Director on Unity).**

**What they do**
- **Mind-of-Director** (2026) [read] runs in Unity on Timeline tracks. A character's behaviour per clip is a small tuple: physical state, position, facing, at start and end. Cameras are not raw transforms. They are a library of 12 single-person, 8 two-person and 1 group shot categories, each a parameterized function `T_k(Θ_k)` of viewpoint, distance and angle, with usage rules and tags. The LLM picks a template, two cinematographer agents debate, a director agent decides, and the engine checks collisions and occlusion and feeds that back until the shot passes.
- **The Virtual Cinematographer** (He, Cohen, Salesin, SIGGRAPH 1996) [excerpt] encoded film idioms as hierarchical state machines. It selects from a small set of camera setups computed in closed form, and the camera modules subtly nudge actors to frame each shot. So the idea of nudging the actor to suit the frame is 30 years old and proven.
- **Remotion** [read] and Motion Canvas gain the same thing from composition: reusable components with local time. The LLM plans and a deterministic renderer executes.

**Sources**
- https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14790v1
- https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=150986
- https://www.remotion.dev/docs/the-fundamentals

**How to adopt**

Add one authoring layer above today's cues, and keep today's cues as the compile target and the escape hatch.

```json
{ "id": "s041", "for": "2bars", "idea": "he realises the remote is stuck to him", "feel": "dread",
  "stage": "sofa_front",
  "cast": { "person": { "mark": "sofa.seat", "perform": "hand_stuck_panic@take3" } },
  "camera": { "template": "mcu_three_quarter", "on": "person.head", "side": "left", "move": "slow_push" },
  "look": "room_tv_cold", "beats": ["22:1 pull", "22:3 yank"] }
```

- Shots use **shot-local time**, so moving a shot means changing the cut and nothing else.
- Actor visibility comes from `cast`. That removes the 971 `hide` and `show` cues by construction.
- Replace the per-part scripts with shared, named, tested templates: `rack_focus`, `hero_reveal`, `reaction_cut`, `crash_zoom`. A template is a function that returns cues, like `macros` today but with parameters, validation and a test.
- Keep a `raw` block on each shot for one-off gags. The aim is that most shots do not need it, not that none do.
- Follow the verification loop: template, then engine check for occlusion and framing, then adjust, before J sees it.

**Limit:** Mind-of-Director says its templates don't capture highly dynamic or unconventional cinematography. J's best moments are unconventional, so the escape hatch must stay first-class.

### 2. Performances are whole takes, chosen best-of-N. Stop assembling bodies from parts.

**Evidence: PROVEN. Every production in this research starts from a whole human or acted performance.**

**What they do**
- **Unity Adam** [excerpt]: captured the actor and camera together at a studio, and hand-keyed the frames where capture failed.
- **Unity Enemies** [read]: cast an actress, rehearsed, and "prototyped the direction by recording her on video first", before committing to production.
- **Epic's Fortnite short** [read]: shot very rough mocap and cut it into Sequencer as shots, so the performance existed in the edit from the first day.
- **Arcane** [read]: no suit, but animators film themselves acting the part and use that as reference. One source describes it as hand-keyed without mocap [excerpt, lower-quality source].

So the source of a good performance is always a whole person doing the whole thing. Corrections come after. `PIPELINE.md` reached the same conclusion from the other side: arms and poses were bad for 10 rounds because bodies were built cue by cue.

**How to adopt: the ladder**

Try each rung in order, and record which one each performance came from.

| Rung | Source | Cost | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Existing library: Mixamo, the Asset Store packs, Quaternius CC0 | Free | Fastest. Filter through finding 5 first. |
| 2 | Kimodo through Gen, `takes` 4 to 8, `library.judge` | About $0.30 to $1 per clip per Gen's doc, needs the port and J's OK | Best for "a person does X" that the library lacks |
| 3 | Video to motion: anyone acts it on a phone, Uthana at $0.05 per second of video [read] | Under $1 for a take | Best for specific, characterful acting. Outputs FBX, GLB or BVH. Commercial use on paid tiers |
| 4 | Text-to-motion through Uthana V3, $0.10 per second, or V2 at $0.03 [read] | Cheap, no GPU | Useful stopgap with a hosted API. Qualitative only, not exact poses |
| 5 | `keys` and bone edits on top of a take | Agent time | For corrections, never as the starting point |

Rung 3 is the one no comparable agent pipeline uses, and it fits J's stated rule: AI is allowed for motion data on our rigs. J never touches Unity, but a 20-second phone clip is direction, not Unity work. That is J's call and should be offered, not assumed. For the dance J called "so bad", a person actually dancing to the track is very likely better than anything assembled.

**Limits**
- Single-camera video mocap is weaker than suits or multi-camera rigs [excerpt], and hand contact is not guaranteed [read]. Grips still need the tested contact from `PIPELINE.md`.
- Kimodo's own limits [read]: 10 seconds per prompt, fewer than 20 constrained frames per constraint type, foot skating without post-processing, constraints not hit exactly, and poor results for actions outside its training set. Multi-prompt chains lose time to transitions. Generate short and cut.

### 3. Editorial first: cut the whole piece before animating anything

**Evidence: PROVEN. Standard practice at Pixar and in real-time previs.**

**What they do**
- **Pixar** [excerpt] screens the film as story reels before animating, and rebuilds them when they fail. *Brave*'s director said he made the film in reel form four times in 18 months. The stated reason is that a sequence that doesn't work as a reel won't be saved by animation.
- **Fortnite** [read]: no traditional storyboard. Whiteboard sketches and rough mocap went straight into Sequencer. Shots could be deleted, added, lengthened or shortened as the edit developed, and notes went into the edit quickly to check flow. Layout built a full assembly before the editor took over. This reduced back-and-forth and improved continuity and screen direction.
- **The Third Floor** [excerpt] extends the same logic: previs, then techvis to prove a shot can be done, then postvis for fast delivery after the shoot.
- **OpenTimelineIO** [excerpt] keeps the cut as a data file that holds order and lengths and only *references* media. TDs splice updated renders into the cut.

**Sources**
- https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/a-fortnite-at-epic/
- https://ktar.com/arizona-news/pixar-filmmaking-a-process-of-alchemy-says-brave-director-mark-andrews
- https://www.cgw.com/Press-Center/Web-Exclusives/2019/Third-Floor-At-the-Forefront-of-Real-Time-Vis.aspx
- https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenTimelineIO

**How to adopt**

`PIPELINE.md` gate 4 is already this. Make it enforceable with three additions.

1. A `cut.json` that is the only place that holds shot order and length. Shots live in their own files. Reordering, trimming or dropping a shot edits one line. Use a plain JSON shape. OTIO itself is optional, worth adding only if J wants the cut in an external editor.
2. Allow **stand-in performances** in the cut. A grey capsule, a library clip or a phone video of J counts. The cut has to read before any polish.
3. Make gag proving the "techvis" step: a risky gag gets a pass or fail test in `mv lab` before it enters the cut. The double, the kaiju and the chorus are the case for this.

The cut needs to survive a change of song or runtime, so anchor shots to named beats where possible, and resolve those to times at compile.

### 4. Layered overrides and takes, so changes never overwrite

**Evidence: PROVEN. This is how USD pipelines and Unreal's takes work.**

**What they do**
- **USD** composes layers. Stronger layers override weaker ones without touching the source, in a fixed order (LIVERPS, formerly LIVRPS) [excerpt from NVIDIA and Foundry docs].
- A typical shot stack is layout, animation, FX, lighting, each with a **sequence-wide and a per-shot version** [excerpt, Foundry]. AYON's default strengths are layout 200, animation 300, simulation 400, FX 500, lighting 600 [excerpt].
- **Variants** let one thing carry several switchable versions. **Inherits** let you change every pine tree in a forest from one source.
- **Unreal Sequencer** [excerpt, Epic docs]: every shot is its own asset, the Take system tries alternatives without modifying the original, and a higher Hierarchical Bias decides which of two sources wins for the same actor.
- **Spider-Verse** [excerpt]: for the sequel, Imageworks built a dedicated, non-destructive layer to resolve the friction between stepped animation and simulation. It was a structural fix, not more patching.

**Sources**
- https://docs.nvidia.com/learn-openusd/latest/composition-basics/strength-ordering.html
- https://learn.foundry.com/katana/Content/rg/usd_nodes/usdsublayeradd.html
- https://help.ayon.app/help/articles/4158006
- https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/sequences-shots-and-takes-in-unreal-engine
- https://history.siggraph.org/learning/fitting-variable-rate-animation-into-a-modern-feature-film-pipeline-by-oskarsson-and-chazot

**How to adopt: copy the idea in JSON, not USD**

```
styles/<style>.json          look for the whole piece (swap to pivot the style)
  sequences/<part>.json      overrides for a part
    shots/s041.json          the shot
      shots/s041.notes.json  J's notes, strongest layer, always wins
```

- Fixed order, later wins, per key. The compiler merges the stack and reports which layer set each final value, so `mv why s041 person.look` answers "where did this come from?"
- **Takes:** `"take":"b"` selects an alternative block in the same shot, and all takes stay on disk. A/B for J becomes one flag and two short renders.
- **Inherit:** a character or prop change flows from one definition. No shot repeats a model path.
- **J's notes as a layer** keep a fix from being lost when a generator or template regenerates. Today, a fix in a generated act gets overwritten by its script.

### 5. Test the motion library before any shot uses it

**Evidence: PROVEN as a practice, in that game studios build and clean a curated set. The automated gate is SPECULATIVE only in its details.**

**What they do**
- Unreal's Motion Matching quality comes from its database: Epic ships a curated locomotion set with loops, pivots and stops [read]. Adding more animation adds options with no extra transition setup.
- Mind-of-Director names the curated library as its quality limit [read].
- Gen's `library.judge` already does best-of-N by vision.

**How to adopt**

Most of this already exists here, run at the wrong time. `motioncheck`, `mv motion`, `limitsMovedDegrees` and `mv lab` all run after a shot exists. Run them when a clip enters the library instead.

- A clip is admitted only after it plays on **our** rig through a test harness: joint limits, pops, foot slide, self-intersection, on-beat quality.
- Each clip gets a card in a machine-readable catalogue: tags, in-place or travelling, contact frames, energy, foot contacts, best blend-in and blend-out frames, tempo if rhythmic, a moving silhouette preview, and a pass or fail.
- Agents can only reference admitted clips from the shot layer.

Doing this for a few hundred clips once replaces the same fix on many shots, and it carries over to every later video. It is the single cheapest way to make the first render good.

### 6. Make every shot independently fast to see, and rebuild only what changed

**Evidence: PROVEN. Playblasts and shot-level work are universal; content-hash caching is standard build practice.**

**What they do**
- Unreal: each shot is a separate sequence asset, and teams work in parallel without conflicts [excerpt].
- Fortnite iterated in real time against the edit instead of waiting on offline renders [read].
- ComfyUI and Houdini Solaris work as graphs of cached nodes, which is why a change near the end costs little. Hawkes Studio's assets lock already records what made each file.
- Ragdoll here already caches: same cue and pose, no re-bake.

**How to adopt**
- Hash each compiled shot from its inputs (its layers, clips, song map). A build touches only shots whose hash changed. Today's plain build is 168 s; a shot-only build should be seconds, though that is my estimate.
- Add a **playblast tier**: one shot, 640x360, no post, no effects, fixed light, 30 fps. That is the review format for performance. Keep final-quality renders for final gates.
- One Unity queue and one builder, as `PIPELINE.md` already plans. Everything that doesn't need Unity runs outside it: lint, template expansion, cut changes.
- On this PC, fast iteration depends on not waiting on the full frame. Budget final renders per shot, and only re-render what changed, then assemble with ffmpeg. Whether a rented GPU could run final renders has not been researched. Hawkes already runs RunPod workers for playtests, so it is worth a look later.

## Tier 2: strong improvements to quality. Do during or right after the rebuild.

### 7. Inertialization instead of crossfades

**Evidence: PROVEN. Shipped in Gears of War and now standard in game engines.**

**What they do:** At a transition, record the offset between old and new pose (position and velocity), play only the new clip, and decay the offset to zero with a critically damped spring. Velocity stays continuous and the new clip is the truth at once. A crossfade averages two motions for the whole blend, which is where mushy poses and foot slide come from.

**Sources**
- https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call [read]
- https://gdcvault.com/play/1025165/Inertialization-High-Performance-Animation-Transitions [excerpt]

**How to adopt:** It fits `MVPerform` well, because the decay has an exact closed form. The offset can be computed from the two clips at the cue's start time, then evaluated at any later time, so it stays a pure function of the timeline and will pass `reverse_test`. Add it as the default for `anim` transitions, with `blend` setting the half-life. Keep crossfade as an option, because `blendTree` and slow dissolves between very different poses can still want it. I expect it to reduce transition artifacts across the board. It is small, testable and cheap.

### 8. Beat-warp dance and rhythmic clips, and search for the transition

**Evidence: RESEARCH (2003 method, well established in the literature). Our use is SPECULATIVE but simple.**

**What they do**
- **Kim, Park, Shin, SIGGRAPH 2003** [excerpt]: extract beats from example motion, build a transition graph of basic movements, and walk that graph in sync with the music.
- **Motion matching** [read] is a search for the best next pose across a database, using pose and trajectory features, without hand-built transitions.
- **EDGE** (Stanford, CVPR 2023) generates dance from music, with joint-wise conditioning and in-betweening [read]. It needs Jukebox features and 16 GB or more of GPU.

**Sources**
- https://history.siggraph.org/?p=115880
- https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-matching-in-unreal-engine
- https://github.com/Stanford-TML/EDGE

**How to adopt**

Today `beats` locks a whole cycle to a length. It does not align the strong moments of the motion to the music.

1. Offline, detect motion beats per clip (a sharp minimum of joint speed or the foot plant).
2. Warp clip time so those beats land on chosen song hits. Pure and reversible, since it is only a remap of clip time.
3. Build a small transition graph of dance phrases, then pick phrases per bar to fit the song's energy from `song.json`.
4. Bake the result into a clip, the way ragdoll already bakes.

Use the same search to pick blend points: "nearest pose and velocity in clip B to this frame of clip A". That is the useful part of motion matching for a directed film. Do it at build time. A real-time matcher answers a game controller, and nothing here needs that.

`JLPM22/MotionMatching` (MIT, Unity 6+, URP samples, inertialization, foot locking) [read] is worth reading for feature code. It is described as a work in progress, so borrow from it and don't depend on it.

### 9. Animate on twos, per actor, as a style tool

**Evidence: PROVEN (Spider-Verse, Arcane effects).**

**What they do**
- **Spider-Verse** was mostly on twos, at times on threes [read], and up to sixes [excerpt]. The rate varied by emotion, by character and even by body part [excerpt]. Too-smooth, too-splined motion looked wrong to the team, and poses held as long as needed [read].
- Hair and cloth simulate on ones or they jump around [excerpt]. **StepSets** let later departments republish a scene at a different frame rate and generate FX and sims from that version [excerpt].
- Instead of motion blur they used overlaid lines and a rolling shutter effect [read].
- **Arcane** puts 2D effects on twos over 3D [excerpt].

**Sources**
- https://www.fxguide.com/?p=244678
- https://vfxvoice.com/?p=5908138
- https://history.siggraph.org/learning/fitting-variable-rate-animation-into-a-modern-feature-film-pipeline-by-oskarsson-and-chazot

**How to adopt**
- Today's `steps` applies to the whole world. Add `steps` per actor and per bone group, as a shot or style layer setting. The engine is a pure function of time, so this is only a quantized time per actor.
- Keep the camera, simulation and particles on ones unless a look asks otherwise.
- Stepped motion needs strong poses, or it reads as dropped frames. Pair it with the `keys` holds from finding 2, and turn off motion blur on those actors.
- It is also a real pivot lever: "make this one graphic" can be one style setting.

**Limit:** Spider-Verse animators averaged about one second a week against the usual four [read]. Stepping doesn't give that look by itself. It works on strong acting, and it exposes weak mocap as readily as it hides it. Test on one shot before adopting widely.

### 10. Styles as swappable layers, built for a weak GPU

**Evidence: PROVEN for the techniques. Mapping them to integrated graphics is SPECULATIVE.**

**What they do**
- **Arcane** [excerpt]: 3D characters, painted textures, lighting painted into the textures, backgrounds as projected paintings on simple 3D geometry, and a small team of 2D effects artists on top. Musical segments switch style for each song [read].
- **Spider-Verse** [read]: each frame should read as a painting, with hand-drawn effects and treatment per camera.

**Sources**
- https://vfxvoice.com/?p=13145207
- https://www.creativebloq.com/art/2d-animation/how-the-creative-team-behind-netflixs-arcane-pushed-the-animation-even-further-for-the-second-season

**How to adopt**
- A style is a file: grade, stylize pass, step rate, line settings, light rig defaults. TK Shock already has the pieces (`toon`, `ink`, `hatch`, `halftone`).
- Painted and baked lighting is cheap at render time, which suits this PC better than real-time shadows. Gen can generate textures and painted backdrops, and J's rule allows AI textures. A painting projected on 3D geometry should count as a texture under that rule, but confirm with J.
- Different styles per piece of a video is itself a technique, and a cheap way to give a short or trailer identity.

### 11. Parameterized camera grammar with a verification loop

**Evidence: PROVEN for composition zones and idioms. RESEARCH for the agent loop.**

**What they do:** Cinemachine's composer [excerpt, Unity docs] defines dead and soft zones in the frame. Mind-of-Director verifies framing, collision and occlusion in the engine and retries [read]. Camera modules can nudge actors for the frame [excerpt].

**Sources**
- https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@2.9/api/Cinemachine.CinemachineComposer.html
- https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14790v1

**How to adopt:** The baked dead zone is already the right idea, and Cinemachine's run-time damping depends on past frames, so keep baking. Add a shot-size vocabulary (ECU, CU, MCU, MS, WS), the 180-degree line per scene, and screen-direction checks. `REVIEW.md` flagged line-crossing by hand; a linter can catch it. Add a pre-render check: subject on screen, not occluded, head room, gaze room. Express shot size by what the frame shows ("frame from chest up"), and solve position from the character's bones and the lens. That keeps framing correct when the character, the set or the blocking changes.

### 12. Separate reviewer, independent of the animator

**Evidence: RESEARCH for agents. PROVEN for the human equivalent (dailies).**

**What they do:** FilmAgent runs crew-role agents with iterative feedback, and human evaluators rated it above single-agent baselines, 3.98 of 5 on average [read]. Mind-of-Director adds debating cinematographers, and a director decision [read]. MotionCritic (ICLR 2025) learns motion quality from human judgement and beats earlier metrics [excerpt].

**Sources**
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12909
- https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/29694

**How to adopt:** `PIPELINE.md` already plans this. Add a fixed checklist per gate, and a **reviewer that sees motion**: a contact sheet from the playblast plus `motioncheck` numbers, never one still. Feed each confirmed J note back as a lint rule or a check, so the same failure can't reach J twice. MotionCritic is worth a later trial as an automatic ranking signal for takes, but it needs a SMPL-style motion input and hasn't been tested on our rig. Note that the "FilmAgent" GitHub repo now shows a different project (VideoClaw), so use the paper.

## Tier 3: useful later or for specific formats

### 13. Cascadeur for hero-shot cleanup

**Evidence: PROVEN as a tool. Automating it here is SPECULATIVE.**

Cascadeur offers AutoPosing, AutoPhysics, AI inbetweening and animation unbaking, which turns baked mocap into editable keys [excerpt]. Its API has `call_action` for running menu commands, including FBX export [excerpt]. The free version is non-commercial and doesn't export FBX. Indie is about $99 a year under $100k revenue [excerpt]. I could not confirm headless use or which tiers have the Python API.

Sources: https://cascadeur.com/help/category/279, https://cgpress.org/archives/cascadeur-2024-1-introduces-animation-unbaking.html

Adopt only after a trial. It is installed on this PC, per `AGENTS.md`. It is a desktop app, so an agent would have to drive it through its Python API with the GUI open. Try one baked take through unbake and AutoPhysics. If the result is clearly better than our spring overlap, wire it in as a build step for hero shots. Check the license tier before any commercial release. Do not build the pipeline around it.

### 14. Secondary motion on hair and cloth

**Evidence: PROVEN in general. Fit with the pure-function rule is SPECULATIVE.**

Epic's Fortnite short added secondary motion with Anim Dynamics [read]. Magica Cloth 2 is a Unity DOTS option for BoneCloth and MeshCloth on URP [excerpt], but I found nothing on baking or Timeline use, and it can't be applied to vertices deformed by blend shapes [excerpt]. Anything frame-dependent breaks scrubbing. So simulate at build time, bake to a clip, and cache, the same as ragdoll. `MVOverlap` already covers much of this and is pure.

### 15. Lip sync and face

**Evidence: PROVEN tools.**

`tools/lipsync.py` exists and produced 161 cues, so low priority. Rhubarb analyzes a recorded file offline and outputs about ten mouth shapes [excerpt]. uLipSync is MIT, MFCC-based and runs on Burst [excerpt]. Bake either one to curves. Epic's short built faces from audio alone [read]. Enemies' 4D face capture is impractical for anything larger, per a former Unity team member [excerpt], so don't chase it. Layer blinks and brow accents on phrase boundaries through templates.

### 16. Explainers and motion graphics on a second backend

**Evidence: PROVEN for Remotion's model and agent support.**

Remotion renders a frame number to a canvas [read], the same pure-function idea as this engine. It has official agent skills, including captions and maps [read]. It also renders React Three content, but one comparison notes that realistic human motion is outside what it does [excerpt], and a licence applies above a company revenue threshold [excerpt]. Check the current terms.

Sources: https://www.remotion.dev/docs/the-fundamentals, https://www.remotion.dev/docs/ai/skills

Make `cut.json` engine-neutral: each shot names a backend, `unity` or `remotion`. Unity renders 3D shots and Remotion renders diagrams, titles and captions, and ffmpeg assembles them. An explainer can then be mostly Remotion with a few 3D inserts. It costs very little to allow this in the format now, so do that, and build the Remotion backend only when an explainer is real work.

Motion Canvas is similar, but its generator style is harder for agents [excerpt], so prefer Remotion. Manim suits math specifically.

### 17. Interactive experiences

**Evidence: PROVEN for branching tools. Connecting them to this compiler is SPECULATIVE.**

Ink is an MIT narrative language that compiles to JSON, with a C# runtime, a JS port and a Unity plugin [read]. Yarn Spinner's `<<command>>` lines call into game code, which maps directly to cues [read].

Sources: https://github.com/inkle/ink, https://docs.yarnspinner.dev/write-yarn-scripts/scripting-fundamentals/commands

The shot structure from finding 1 is what makes this possible: shots with local time are playable units, so a branch is a choice between shot IDs. Keep to linear for now, but don't let shots depend on global song time or on the shot before them, except through a declared hand-off pose. Music videos are beat-locked and interactive pieces aren't, so the compiler needs a "free time" mode. Don't build this until a real project needs it.

### 18. Agent-readable previs from a script

**Evidence: RESEARCH.**

Script2Screen and Mind-of-Director turn scripts into editable 3D previews [excerpt, read]. One thesis reports about 85% faster animatics in Blender, unverified [excerpt]. The gain here is a brief-to-beat-sheet-to-cut generator that uses templates and stand-ins. This comes for free once findings 1 and 3 exist.

## Considered and not recommended

| Option | Why not, for now |
|---|---|
| Move to Unreal | Rewrites the compiler, engine and checks, and this PC can't run it well. Take the ideas: takes, shots, layered tracks |
| Real USD in Unity | Adds a heavy dependency to gain what a JSON merge gives us. Only one tool reads the data. Reconsider if the pipeline spreads across DCCs |
| Unity Sequences package | The newest manual found is 2.1 from 2023, and I could not confirm it is maintained [excerpt]. Our compiler generates Timelines, so its folder setup adds little |
| Real-time motion matching on the timeline | Stateful and built for controller input. Conflicts with the pure-function rule. Use the search offline (finding 8) |
| Learned Motion Matching | It reduces memory use in shipped games [excerpt]. We have no such problem |
| 4D face capture | Impractical at scale, even for Unity's own team [excerpt] |
| HY-Motion as a primary | At least 24 GB VRAM, no multi-person, no loop or in-place modes [read]. Gen's doc notes license terms that exclude the EU, UK and South Korea. Keep as Gen's fallback |
| EDGE on our rig | Code is MIT, but it needs Jukebox features and 16 GB or more of GPU, and its SMPL output needs the same body-model care as Gen's doc notes. A person dancing costs less |
| GVHMR self-hosted | License text not confirmed. Hosted video to motion at $0.05 per second is simpler |
| BONES-SEED as a library | 142,220 motions including 11,006 dances, BVH on SOMA [read], which is very attractive. But the licence is gated and the card implies commercial use needs a separate deal [read]. **Read the licence before touching it.** The Kimodo SOMA-SEED model is a separate licence. Worth emailing licensing@bones.studio if J wants it |
| Move.ai, DeepMotion, Rokoko Vision | Only vendor and affiliate pages found, no hard pricing or benchmarks [excerpt]. Uthana has public per-second rates and an API. Compare them on one real take if rung 3 proves useful |

## Suggested order

1. Files: `cut.json`, shot files, layer merge, takes, shot hash. No new look, same compile target. Prove by rebuilding one TK Shock part from shots.
2. Motion library gate and clip catalogue.
3. Inertialization default.
4. Shot templates, derived from the moves that worked in TK Shock.
5. Gen: move `motion.from-text` off SSH. Needs a separate decision from J, as GPU work costs money.
6. Beat-warp for dance, per-actor steps, style files.

## Gaps in this research

- Unity's Adam animation blog post returned 404, so Adam comes from search excerpts only.
- I found no primary account of The Third Floor's current pipeline. Sources run 2018 to 2020.
- I found no write-up of Fortnite's newer shorts on UEFN, MetaHuman Animator or Control Rig. Findings use the 2017 short.
- Pixar's reel count is one director's statement on one film. Nothing shows a studio-wide number.
- Arcane's "no mocap" and "painted lighting" come from secondary sources. The VFX Voice piece I read does not cover animation reference, painted-texture detail or frame rates.
- Nothing here measures how much any adoption will reduce iterations on this pipeline. Findings 1 to 6 should be tested on one rebuilt part, with the number of rounds counted.
